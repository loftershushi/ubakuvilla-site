One month in Biei: choosing a long stay in the Furano and Biei area

Instead of "traveling", you can choose "living". Stay for a month at a private villa in the Biei and Furano area. Walk through the mist on the hills in the morning, cook in the kitchen at noon, and read quietly surrounded by nature at night. Here is everyday Biei, which sightseeing never reaches.

Why a month in Biei?

Biei is a place that grows deeper the longer you stay. There are sights that three days of classic spots will never show you: fields in the morning mist, a farmer's small truck on the road at dusk, the color of the sky at the moment the season turns. The only way to know them is simply to stay longer.

With remote work now common, more people can work from anywhere. Taking a "trip to live" by the month has become a realistic choice.

A model plan for one month

WEEK 1 │ Getting to know Biei

Don't rush after you arrive. Spend the first week getting used to the air of Biei. Take your time visiting the classic spots, such as Shikisai-no-Oka, the Blue Pond and the Patchwork Road. Stop at the mysterious blue of Shirahige Falls, and take in the whole sweep of the hills from the observation deck at Hokusei-no-Oka Observation Park. Drive the ups and downs of the Jet Coaster Road and feel Biei's landscape with your whole body.

When you tire of sightseeing, read at your leisure in Biei Town Library. Spend a quiet moment at Takushinkan Photo Gallery with the Biei landscapes Shinzo Maeda photographed over many years. In the late afternoon, enjoy time in the forest at KONON Cafe. Some days you may want to get moving at the town sports center or the Waiwai Pool. Learning about Biei's history and culture at the local history museum (丘のまち郷土学館) changes the way you see the landscape a little.

Use this week to build a daily rhythm of shopping at Fuji Supermarket and cooking for yourself.

WEEK 2 │ Out to Furano and Asahikawa

A week for exploring northern Hokkaido widely from your base in Biei. In Furano, visit the lavender fields at Farm Tomita, the forest of Ningle Terrace and the lavender of Hinode Park. Canoeing and camping at Lake Kanayama are summer experiences too. Or spend an active day rafting down the Sorachi River with Raft&Crew.

In Asahikawa, see the behavioral exhibits at Asahiyama Zoo, take your time with furniture and crafts made from Hokkaido wood at the Asahikawa Design Center, and meet Hokkaido's local sake on a tour and tasting at Otokoyama Sake Brewery. Because your base is a private villa, you can leave your luggage behind and move around freely.

WEEK 3 │ Exploring Higashikawa

Higashikawa, the town next to Biei, is a rich town known to those in the know. Climbing or hiking Mt. Asahidake is a day to feel Hokkaido's great nature with your whole body. At 2,291 m, Asahidake is covered in alpine flowers even in summer.

Along the Craft Road you will find distinctive workshops for woodwork, pottery and glass. At Kitanosumaisekkeisha you can experience furniture and spaces made with Hokkaido wood. Spend a quiet afternoon in the library and gallery of Sento Pure II, and shop for local food at the roadside station Michi-no-Eki Higashikawa Michikusakan. Stop by Higashikawa Shrine, and meet local people at the Higashikawa community center. In the evening, unwind at Kitoushi Forest Kitoron, a hot bath facility supervised by Kengo Kuma, and have dinner there too.

WEEK 4 │ Finishing at your own pace

In the last week of the month, go back to your favorite places once more: Takushinkan in the morning, the Patchwork Road at dusk, the last slot at KONON Cafe. You will surely have found a place or two that makes you think, "I'll come back." A week to look back slowly on time you could only have spent in Biei.

Start work after a walk on the morning hills, and head to the Blue Pond in the evening. In Biei, that rhythm comes naturally.
